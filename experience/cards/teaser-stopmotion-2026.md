# Particleworks Experience 2026 — 10s Cardboard Stop-Motion Teaser

A **real, hand-shot cardboard stop-motion** teaser (no AI, no CGI). The whole film is built from
**corrugated cardboard, kraft paper and paper chips** — a warm, handmade "engineered by hand" world.
The visual pun still lands: Particleworks simulates fluids as **particles**, so here the hero motion is a
swarm of **little cardboard chips** that flow, swirl and re-assemble into shapes — a gear, the **Modena
skyline**, and a final **cardboard tortellino** 🥟.

Everything you see is paper/board. The charm is the texture: visible corrugation flutes, torn kraft
edges, pencil marks left in, a faint drop-shadow under every piece.

- **Length:** exactly **10.00 s**
- **Frame plan:** **12 fps, shot on twos → 120 unique frames total** (24 frames per 2 s beat).
  Every beat below is broken to the frame so the 10 s land precisely.
- **Structure:** **5 beats × 2.00 s** (frames 1–24, 25–48, 49–72, 73–96, 97–120).
- **Aspect:** **1:1** master (LinkedIn feed); shoot ~15% extra headroom for a **9:16** reframe.

> Timing key: at 12 fps, frame *n* starts at **(n−1)/12 s**. Beat edges fall on frames
> 1 / 25 / 49 / 73 / 97 → **0.00 / 2.00 / 4.00 / 6.00 / 8.00 s**, end on frame 120 → **10.00 s**.

## THE LOOK
- **Base surface:** flat kraft-paper table (warm tan), lightly crumpled then re-flattened for texture.
- **Hero board:** single-wall corrugated cardboard, cut so the **flutes read as a subtle grain**.
- **Palette (paint the board, keep the texture):**
  - **Coral/red** = hero brand pieces (matches `card-00-program.png`).
  - **Electric-green** = accent chips and the underline (used sparingly, placed by hand).
  - **Natural kraft / cream** = "sky", food and the Modena beats.
- One clean directional light, soft real shadow, minimal and premium. Leave a few honest imperfections
  (a torn corner, a pencil guide line) — that's the point of cardboard.

## RIG & SETUP (top-down for particle beats, low-angle for pop-ups)
- **Camera locked overhead** on a copy-stand for Beats 1–3 (the flowing-chip beats). For Beats 4–5,
  either keep top-down or tilt to a **shallow 20–30° low angle** so the pop-up skyline and tortellino
  stand up in relief. Decide once and lock it — don't move mid-morph.
- **Manual everything:** manual focus, manual exposure, manual white balance. No auto, no flicker,
  no window light. One softbox at ~30° + a white bounce card opposite.
- **Software:** Dragonframe (or Stop Motion Studio). **Onion-skin every frame**; nudge chips **1–3 mm**
  at a time.
- **Registration:** tape a hidden grid under the kraft sheet; mark camera, light and stencil positions.
  If the table shifts, the morph breaks.

## MATERIALS (the "particles" and the builds)
- **Chips (the particles):** ~6–10 mm discs/squares **punched from painted cardboard** — a big pile in
  coral, a small jar in green, a jar in cream. A craft hole-punch makes hundreds fast.
- **Stencils / guides:** laser-cut or knife-cut cardboard **stencils** of each target — the Particleworks
  pictogram, a **gear**, an **oil droplet**, the **Ghirlandina + Duomo skyline**, the **tortellino**.
  Place them *under* a glass sheet as a build guide, or shoot just inside pencil outlines.
- **Wordmark & text:** **cut cardboard letters** (or clean type added in post — see notes). Keep a coral
  set and a cream set.
- **Pop-up pieces:** skyline and tortellino as **layered cardboard cutouts on paper hinges** so they can
  fold flat → stand up over a few frames (classic pop-up-book move).
- **Hidden rigging:** thin **wire or a magnet under the sheet** to slide the green droplet cleanly; tiny
  **cardboard shims** to prop standing pieces; tweezers + soft brush for placing chips.

## TECHNIQUE NOTES (cardboard specifics)
- **Shoot morphs in reverse.** It's far easier to *deconstruct* a finished shape (sweep chips away) than
  to build one blind. Capture the teardown, reverse in edit → flawless "assembly." Applies to Beats 1–3.
- **Replacement pieces for cracks & folds.** Pre-cut the droplet-crack stages and the tortellino fold
  stages as separate cardboard parts; swap them in rather than bending one piece — cleaner and repeatable.
- **Pop-ups on paper hinges.** Score fold lines with a dead ballpoint so the board hinges on the same line
  every take. Prop with hidden cardboard shims; raise a few degrees per frame for a smooth stand-up.
- **Keep the texture lit.** Rake the key light slightly along the corrugation so the flutes and torn edges
  read — that grain is what sells "cardboard." Watch that standing pieces don't throw shadow on the mark.
- **Twos, not ones.** 12 fps on twos gives the tactile handmade cadence and halves shooting (120 frames).
  Go to ones only on the fastest chip bursts (Beat 3 f49–52) if they strobe.
- **Text & CTA in post (optional).** You can shoot real cardboard letters for charm, *or* shoot clean
  builds and add the wordmark/lower-thirds/CTA as crisp type in edit (heavy geometric sans, white/coral +
  a green underline) so small text stays legible on feed.
- **Count check:** the lower-third now reads **11** talks (Börger FlowSep added) — keep it consistent with
  the cards and program page.
